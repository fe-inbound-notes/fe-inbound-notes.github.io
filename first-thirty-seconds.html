The First 30 Seconds of an Inbound Final Expense Call

The first half minute of an inbound call does more work than most agents think. The person on the other end just saw an ad, found a number, and dialed it. They do not know you. They may not remember the brand name in the commercial. They are deciding, almost immediately, whether they reached a real person who can help or another recording.

You cannot control the ad they saw. You can control how you sound when you pick up, what you say first, and whether you give them a reason to stay.

Pick up as if you expected the call

Answer on an early ring if you can. A long ring after someone has already waited through a prompt makes the caller start irritated. If you need a second to mute a notification or pull up your quoting tool, take that second before you click accept, not after you have said hello.

Say your name and your role in one breath. "Hi, this is Maria, a licensed insurance agent, how can I help you today?" is enough. You do not need a company paragraph or a list of license numbers in the greeting. If they ask who you are with, answer plainly.

Smile when you say it, even if that feels silly. People hear a smile in your voice, and they hear if you are distracted. The caller will copy your pace, so speak at a moderate speed.

Confirm why they called, then ask one easy question

A surprising number of inbound callers start with "I don't know why you people keep calling me." Sometimes they dialed, then forgot. Sometimes a family member handed them the phone. Sometimes they are thinking of a different offer.

Do not argue about who placed the call. Try a calm reset: "You reached a licensed agent who helps with final expense and burial coverage. Did you call about a policy for yourself, or for someone in the family?" That question tells them what you do, asks who the coverage is for, and gives them a simple choice instead of a blank silence.

If they say it is for themselves, stay with them. If they say it is for a parent or a spouse, find out whether that person is there and able to talk. You will need the insured person's information to do real work. A well meaning child can start the call, but you should not pretend you can finish an application without the right person.

Get permission to continue

After the first answer, ask if it is a reasonable time to talk. "Do you have about ten minutes so I can ask a few questions and see what you might qualify for?" respects their day and sets a time expectation. If they are in a store, a parking lot, or about to go into an appointment, do not push through a full interview. Confirm a name, confirm a number, agree on a time, and end politely.

If you are straining to hear them, say so and ask whether they can move to a quieter spot or take you off speaker. Ask once, kindly. Asking three times in a row makes people defensive.

What to do when the opening goes sideways

If the person is angry, let them finish a sentence, then acknowledge it without taking the blame for an ad you did not write. "I'm sorry the process has been frustrating. I'm a live agent, and I can help if you'd like to keep going." If they want to be removed from future calls, follow your shop's process for a do-not-contact request. Do not try to turn that moment into a pitch.

If they only wanted a price, and they want it now, do not invent a number. Tell them a real quote depends on age, health, state, and the amount of coverage, and that you can get them a figure once you have those basics. Then ask age or state. The first 30 seconds only need to keep the call from ending on a guess.

If you get silence after "hello," wait a beat and try once more with your name. Auto connections sometimes beat the caller to the line. A second greeting is normal. If no one is there after that, follow your vendor's rule for a short wait and a clean disconnect.

A short opening you can actually use

Here is a compact version you can adapt in your own words:

Hi, this is [Name], a licensed insurance agent. You reached us about final expense coverage. Is this for you, or for a family member? Great. Do you have about ten minutes so I can ask a few questions and get you accurate information?

Then stop talking. Let them answer. The first 30 seconds fail most often when the agent fills every gap with more biography, more disclaimers, or more product names. Ask, listen, and only then begin the interview. Once they have agreed to talk, move to who the policy is for, their state, their age, and the reason they called today. Keep your tone the same as the greeting.

The opening will not close a policy. It only decides whether you get the chance to do the rest of your job. Practice it out loud a few times before your next shift. When the phone rings, you should not be inventing your first sentence.
